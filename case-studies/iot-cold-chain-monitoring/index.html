Situation

Manual logging, GDP non-conformances, and a €47K incident on record

The client distributed temperature-sensitive pharmaceuticals — insulins, biologics, and cold-chain vaccines — across six dedicated cold rooms ranging from 2–8°C (refrigerated) to -20°C (frozen). Monitoring relied entirely on staff manually recording temperatures on paper logs twice per shift.

A GDP (Good Distribution Practice) audit had flagged incomplete records and a 3-hour excursion event that went undetected overnight. The excursion resulted in a €47,000 stock write-off and a formal non-conformance report. The distributor faced either a follow-up audit failure — risking their WDA(H) licence — or a demonstrable remediation.

They called us. Our brief: automated, auditable, real-time temperature monitoring across all six cold rooms, with alerting fast enough to prevent stock loss, not just document it after the fact.

The Problems

No real-time visibility

A temperature excursion at 02:00 would not be discovered until the 06:00 morning round. A four-hour window at +12°C is enough to compromise an entire cold room of biologics.

GDP audit non-conformance

Manual paper logs failed GDP Annex requirements for automated continuous recording. The facility was at risk of licence action at the next inspection.

No network redundancy

The cold storage area had a single Wi-Fi access point with no failover. A router reboot during a network upgrade had previously caused a 45-minute monitoring gap.

No audit trail export

Preparing temperature records for a GDP audit took 4–6 hours of manual compilation per quarter. Inspectors wanted 12-month CSV exports — which didn't exist in any system.

Deployment Timeline

From site survey to production in four weeks

Week 1

Site survey and sensor placement design

Mapped cold room layouts, RF propagation paths, and door cycle frequencies. Specified sensor placement to capture worst-case warm spots near door seals. Documented 4G signal strength for failover planning.

Week 2

Firmware development and backend provisioning

ESP32-S3 firmware written in C with DS18B20 one-wire temperature and SHT31 humidity sensing. MQTT over TLS 1.3 to AWS IoT Core Frankfurt. Spring Boot alerting service and TimescaleDB hypertables provisioned. Alert threshold rules configured per room per GDP specification.

Week 3

Hardware installation and 4G failover commissioning

Sensor nodes installed in all six cold rooms. 4G SIM failover activated — each node independently capable of routing MQTT through mobile data if Wi-Fi fails. Failover tested under controlled Wi-Fi outage: seamless reconnection in under 8 seconds.

Week 4

Dashboard launch, alerting validation, and staff training

React dashboard deployed with live temperature graphs, excursion history, and one-click GDP report export. WhatsApp, SMS, and email alerting validated end-to-end. Three 2-hour training sessions for warehouse and QA staff. GDP documentation package delivered.

Architecture

Built for reliability, not just connectivity

Edge: ESP32-S3 sensor nodes

Each node runs custom C firmware on an ESP32-S3. DS18B20 probes handle temperature (±0.5°C accuracy, calibrated against reference standards), with SHT31 for humidity in rooms where condensation risk is relevant. Readings are taken every 30 seconds and published via MQTT over TLS 1.3 to port 8883.

Connectivity: dual-path with 4G failover

Primary path is the facility Wi-Fi. Each node carries a SIM with 4G data — if Wi-Fi association is lost for more than 15 seconds, the node automatically switches to cellular. AWS IoT Core sees continuous telemetry regardless of local network state. The failover has triggered three times in 14 months; all three were transparent to the dashboard.

Cloud: AWS IoT Core Frankfurt + TimescaleDB

All telemetry is retained in TimescaleDB hypertables with automatic compression after 30 days. Continuous aggregates generate hourly and daily summary views for reporting. Data remains in Frankfurt (eu-central-1) in compliance with GDPR and GDP data localisation requirements.

Alerting: 90-second end-to-end

The Spring Boot alerting service evaluates each MQTT message against per-room threshold rules. Excursions trigger a multi-channel alert: WhatsApp Business API first (highest attention rate), followed by SMS as backup, and email for the audit log. Mean time from excursion to first notification: 90 seconds.

Compliance Outputs

GDP Annex continuous recording

30-second interval logging satisfies GDP Annex requirements for automated temperature monitoring and eliminates the need for manual paper logs.

HACCP report automation

One-click export generates HACCP-formatted CSV reports covering any date range, with digital signatures. Audit preparation reduced from 6 hours to 4 minutes.

Calibration records

Each sensor's calibration certificate is stored against its device ID. Replacement sensors trigger an automated recalibration workflow notification.
