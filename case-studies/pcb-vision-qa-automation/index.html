The Problem

Manual Inspection: Fast, Cheap, and Dangerously Unreliable

A PCB contract manufacturer in the Rhine-Ruhr region was running a manual inspection station at the end of their SMT assembly line. Four operators, one per shift, inspecting boards visually with a loupe and reference card. Cycle time: 12–18 seconds per board. Defect escape rate: 2.3%.

At 2.3%, approximately 1 in 43 boards with a latent defect was shipping to customers. For consumer electronics, this meant warranty claims. For industrial clients, it meant field failures in environments where the board could not easily be serviced. One automotive Tier-2 customer had issued a formal supplier quality warning after three field returns in six months.

The human eye fatigues. After 90 minutes of inspection, miss rate climbs by 40%. The most dangerous shift is the last hour before break — when boards are shipping fastest and attention is lowest.

Technical Implementation

The Architecture

The system uses a fixed 4K camera array mounted above the conveyor inspection point, triggered by a photoelectric sensor as each board enters the inspection zone. Images are captured at four angles for solder joint depth analysis.

A YOLOv8-based detection model, fine-tuned on 14,000 labelled images from the customer's own production history, runs inference on an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX edge device — no cloud dependency, sub-100ms inference per frame. The model detects 23 defect classes including solder bridges, tombstoning, missing components, lifted pins, and polarity errors.

A Python-based QA management application integrates with the customer's ERP for board serialisation, stores all inspection images and results in a PostgreSQL database, and generates ISO 9001-aligned quality records automatically. A web dashboard gives the quality manager real-time line health visibility.
